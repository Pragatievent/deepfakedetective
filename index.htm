What is AI-Generated Content?

Artificial Intelligence generated content (Generative AI) refers to algorithms (such as GANs and Diffusion models) that create brand new text, images, audio, or video based on massive amounts of training data.

Key Takeaway:

Unlike traditional computer graphics created manually by artists, generative AI predicts and synthesizes pixels or sound waves automatically.

What are Deepfakes?

Deepfakes are a specific subset of synthetic media where a person in an existing image or video is replaced with someone else's likeness using deep learning neural networks.

Types of Deepfakes

1. Face Swaps

Overlays one person's facial features onto another body in video footage.

2. Voice Clones (Audio)

Mimics a specific human voice using only a few seconds of audio samples.

3. Puppet Avatars

Drives a still photograph using a live speaker's facial movements.

Scams & Fraud

Financial scams utilizing live voice cloning or video calls to deceive employees into unauthorized money transfers or impersonate family members in distress.

Identity Misuse

The unauthorized use of private citizens' or public figures' likenesses for non-consensual imagery, harassment, or malicious impersonation.

Social Impact

Erosion of trust in digital media, leading to the "Liar's Dividend"—where guilty individuals can dismiss authentic evidence of misconduct as "just a deepfake."

Investigator Field Card

  • Check the Eyes & Blinks: Unnatural pauses or robotic blinking frequencies.
  • Inspect Skin & Jawline: Look for subtle blurring or color blending artifacts.
  • Evaluate Lighting: Do shadows match environmental light sources?
  • Listen to Audio: Check for metallic artifacts, room tone drops, or lip-sync delay.
  • Trace the Source: Can trusted journalism outlets confirm this story?

Verification Guide

Follow these steps when you encounter suspicious media online:

  1. Reverse Image Search: Check if the image appears elsewhere in legitimate news archives.
  2. Inspect Details: Look closely at ears, teeth, glasses frames, and background text.
  3. Verify the Source: Look for confirmation from mainstream, trusted journalism organizations.

What to Do If You See a Suspected Deepfake

Encountered a piece of synthetic media that looks misleading or malicious? Follow these immediate investigator steps:

1. Do Not Share

Sharing unverified media—even to debunk it—amplifies its reach and feeds algorithmic engagement loops.

2. Report to Platform

Use the platform's reporting tool to flag the content under "Misinformation", "Impersonation", or "Synthetic Media".

3. Inform the Subject

If a private individual or public entity is being impersonated, notify them or relevant authorities immediately.
